# A quiz can be "correct" and still test nothing

**What broke:** a content-quality audit of the 239-question bank found the correct answer sat at `correctIndex: 0` for every single question, with no exceptions - tapping the first option every time scored 100% forever, without reading a single scenario. Two more compounding tells made it worse even for someone ignoring position: 98.7% of the time the longer option (by character count) was the correct one, and 69% of wrong options used a dead-giveaway absolutist word ("always", "never", "automatically", "regardless", "entirely", "indefinitely") versus only 14% of correct options. In the worst 20 questions, the correct option was a long, specific "check the contract for X" answer and the wrong option was a short "X is always/never true" strawman - a pattern nobody would actually believe, let alone get tempted by.

**Why it happened:** each question was authored as "here's the right judgment call, and here's an obviously-wrong strawman to contrast it with" rather than "here are two positions a real contract administrator might plausibly take, one of which is right." That authoring habit is invisible reading one question at a time - the strawman looks like reasonable filler - but it's a hard pattern to avoid at scale without checking for it explicitly, since every individual question still reads fine in isolation.

**The fix:** rewrote the bank so (a) which index holds the correct answer is roughly evenly split rather than constant, (b) option lengths are comparable rather than the longer one being a reliable tell, and (c) wrong options are phrased as genuinely tempting mistakes (misapplying a general rule to this specific fact pattern, confusing two similar doctrines, a superficially-reasonable but wrong analogy) instead of absolutist strawmen. Added a regression test (`src/lib/questions.test.ts`) asserting the correct answer isn't at a single fixed position across the whole bank, so this can't silently regress question-by-question the way it silently accumulated in the first place.

**The reusable lesson:** for any dataset of "pick the right one" content (quiz answers, A/B option pairs, multiple-choice anything), audit the *shape* of right vs. wrong answers across the whole set - length, position, phrasing register, tell-words - not just the correctness of each item individually. A dataset where every item is individually defensible can still be trivially gameable in aggregate, and that failure mode is invisible unless you specifically measure for it.
